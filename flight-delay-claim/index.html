Questions

Does my flight qualify?

It can if it took off from an airport in the EU, the UK, Iceland, Norway or Switzerland on any airline, or flew into the EU or UK on an EU or UK airline, and it arrived 3 or more hours late, was canceled less than 14 days before departure, or you were denied boarding. The cause has to be within the airline's control: bad weather, air-traffic-control restrictions and similar extraordinary circumstances don't count. We check all of this for free before anything is filed.

I flew a US airline. Does that count?

Yes, if the flight took off from the EU or UK: a US airline's flight from Paris to New York is covered the same way as a European airline's. A flight from the US to Europe is covered only on an EU or UK airline. Flights within the US aren't covered at all, because US law doesn't require compensation for delays. If a US flight was canceled and you didn't travel, the airline owes you a refund of the ticket; ask it directly.

How much could I get?

The amount is set by law, per passenger: €250 for flights up to 1,500 km, €400 up to 3,500 km (and for longer flights within the EU), and €600 beyond that, or €300 if a long flight arrived between 3 and 4 hours late. UK flights pay £220, £350 and £520 (£260 for 3 to 4 hours late). A direct flight between the US and Europe is in the top band.

Can't I claim this myself for free?

Yes. Airlines have their own claim forms, and doing it yourself is the right choice if you don't mind the paperwork and the follow-up. This is for people who would rather hand it off: we work out the amount, prepare and file the claim, answer the airline's replies and keep chasing. If nothing is paid, it costs you nothing.

Who prepares the claim?

It's drafted with AI tools from your booking details and the flight's record, then checked against the regulation before you see it. You read and approve it before anything is sent, and you can ask for changes.

Do you go to court?

No. We file with the airline, follow up, and take the case to the national enforcement body when an airline stalls or refuses without a valid reason. If it still won't pay, we tell you your options, such as a lawyer or a claim company that litigates, and you owe us nothing.

Will the airline deal with you?

Many airlines accept a claim filed for you once you've signed a one-page authorization, and some also ask for a copy of your ID to check the signature. Others insist that passengers submit claims themselves; for those we prepare the claim and every reply, and you send them from your own email. Either way the airline pays you, not us, and our fee is the same.

How long does it take?

It depends on the airline. Some pay within a few weeks, others take several months, and some refuse claims that look valid. We can't promise a result or a date. We tell you where the claim stands at every step.

My flight was a while ago. Is it too late?

Often not. The time limit depends on the country: commonly two or three years, up to six for UK flights, and shorter in a few places. Send the flight and we'll check.

What happens to my information?

It's used to prepare and file your claim and for nothing else. We don't sell it or share it with anyone but the airline and, if needed, the enforcement body.
